[arXiv260907] Privacy Failure in Split-LLM Training, The Returned Gradient Nullifies the Decoys

arXiv
mlsysllm trainingsplit learning, privacy attack, gradient leakage, decoy rows, differential privacy, clipping, noising
Authors
Georgios Politis, Evangelos Pappas
Institution
Setloop.io

This paper investigates a privacy flaw in a two-node split-LLM training system where decoy rows have zero gradients, allowing the untrusted cloud node to identify real data rows. The authors demonstrate that this leak persists even when forward-channel privacy checks pass, and show that clipping and noising per-row gradients can mitigate the issue at a small cost to model quality. However, they caution that other attack vectors remain unmeasured.

Authors
Ming Xiang, Stratis Ioannidis, Edmund Yeh, Carlee Joe-Wong, Lili Su
Institution
Northeastern University, Carnegie Mellon University

This paper proposes FedSWE, a federated learning algorithm designed to be resilient to heterogeneous and non-stationary client unavailability without requiring prior knowledge of availability dynamics. FedSWE compensates for missed computations, stabilizes global updates, and mixes local updates via implicit gossiping, achieving convergence to stationary points of non-convex objectives with low overhead, as demonstrated by theoretical analysis and numerical experiments.

[arXiv260907] Same Request, Different Answer: Quantization Amplifies Cache-Induced Divergence in LLM Serving

arXiv
mlsysllm inferenceprompt caching, key-value cache, quantization, reproducibility, agentic workload, serving engine
Authors
Aditi Patodiya
Institution
Independent Researcher

This paper studies how prefix caching affects reproducibility in LLM serving, showing that enabling the cache changes agent trajectories in multi-turn workloads, with divergence worsening under weight quantization. Experiments across two engines and four weight formats find that cached execution is deterministic given cache state but irreproducible in practice because the cache state is not part of the request and is never reset by default.

[arXiv260907] Atlas: Optimizing Deployment of Compound AI Workflows on Heterogeneous Clusters

arXiv
mlsysllm inferencecompound AI workflows, model selection, heterogeneous clusters, Markovian Accuracy Predictor, mixed-integer linear programming, execution plan optimization, SLO constraints, distributed inference, profiling, accuracy estimation
Authors
Milos Gravara, Andrija Stanisic, Stefan Nastic
Institution
TU Wien (Distributed Systems Group)

Atlas optimizes deployment of compound AI workflows on heterogeneous clusters by selecting model variants and hardware placements under SLO constraints. It uses a Markovian Accuracy Predictor (MAP) to estimate workflow accuracy from local conditional accuracy transitions, avoiding costly end-to-end profiling, and solves a mixed-integer linear program to maximize predicted accuracy. Experiments show MAP achieves high correlation with true accuracy while reducing profiling cost substantially, and Atlas finds plans near oracle accuracy with up to 42% cost reduction.

Authors

Institution

This paper introduces REACT, a system that operates at the communication library layer to detect network congestion in shared AI clusters and dynamically adjusts the communication collective patterns (e.g., changing aggregation nodes in AllReduce) to mitigate slowdowns, requiring no special network infrastructure support. [arXiv260907] Performance Study of Serverless Workloads in Confidential Virtual Machines

arXiv
syscloud computingconfidential virtual machines, serverless computing, Intel TDX, memory efficiency, warm-start, cold-start, VMEXIT, container consolidation, page deduplication, memory reclaimability
Authors
Rikesh Niroula, Jianchen Shan, Xiaoning Ding
Institution
New Jersey Institute of Technology, Hofstra University

This paper presents an empirical study of serverless workloads running in confidential virtual machines (CVMs), examining memory efficiency and runtime overhead for both warm and cold starts. It finds that CVMs cause significant memory overhead due to disabled page deduplication and reduced reclaimability, and that runtime slowdowns occur for workloads with frequent VMEXITs, worsened by vCPU over-allocation. The study also shows that consolidating multiple containers of the same function in a CVM with selectively relaxed isolation mechanisms can substantially reduce cold-start container creation overhead.

Authors
Ali Zafar Sadiq, Haiying Shen, Masahiro Tanaka
Institution
University of Virginia, Anyscale

CIERA proposes a lossless Allgather communication method for sharded MoE training by exploiting the observation that weight exponents remain stable across iterations, caching them locally and transmitting only sign and mantissa. It applies compression selectively based on net time savings and overlaps compression with communication and computation, achieving significant speedups (e.g., 3.70x over lossless and 3.68x over lossy baselines on OLMoE-1B-7B at 16 GPUs) while preserving bitwise-exact parameter reconstruction.

Authors

Institution
Boston University, University of Kansas

The paper revisits prior claims that MemGuard incurs up to 1.79x slowdowns, showing these overheads were amplified by a suboptimal configuration with an older version where the benchmark was pinned to the master core handling timer interrupts. By reproducing the experiments with a modern, decentralized MemGuard implementation, the authors demonstrate that actual overhead drops significantly and becomes comparable to MemPol, providing updated baseline metrics for future evaluations.

[arXiv260907] BF16 Component-Product Emulation of FP32 and FP64 GEMM on Intel AMX

arXiv
mlsysGPU kernelsIntel AMX, BF16, GEMM emulation, mixed-precision, Ozaki decomposition, component-product decomposition, FP32 emulation, FP64 emulation, oneMKL comparison
Authors
Bing Cui, Yu Liu
Institution
Maginfra Co., Ltd.

This paper proposes a CPU-based method to emulate FP32 and FP64 GEMM using Intel AMX and BF16 matrix products. FP32 is handled by decomposing each operand into three BF16 components and evaluating six component products, while FP64 uses a simplified six-slice Ozaki decomposition with configurable product counts (6, 10, 15, or 21) to trade accuracy for performance. On tested square matrices, the method outperforms oneMKL SGEMM in throughput and can exceed DGEMM for FP64 at sufficiently large orders with fewer retained products.

This paper presents an adaptive progressive compression framework that improves retrieval efficiency for error-bound and PSNR targets by leveraging two complementary interpolation schemes and a novel coefficient decomposition method to exploit spatial correlations among decorrelated data.
