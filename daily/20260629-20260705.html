[arXiv260629] FoggyTrust: Robust Federated Learning with Hierarchical Trust Networks

arXiv
mlsysfault-tolerancefederated learning, Byzantine robustness, fog computing, hierarchical trust networks, FedAdam, SCAFFOLD
Authors
Emmanuel Rassou, Tomas Gonzalez
Institution
Harvard University

The paper proposes FoggyTrust, a hierarchical extension of FLTrust that localizes trust computation to fog nodes to handle globally heterogeneous data while preserving robustness in locally homogeneous client groups. By combining local trust-based aggregation with heterogeneity-aware global optimizers like FedAdam and SCAFFOLD, FoggyTrust achieves over 50% improvement over FLTrust on CIFAR-10 under Krum and Trim attacks, and shows promise for real-world safety-critical settings like distributed wildlife monitoring.

Institution

The paper presents RAMSES, a high-performance computing system designed for secure processing of sensitive data, integrating hardware-based memory encryption, end-to-end file encryption, and multi-layered security measures. Benchmark results from the biomedical sector show that this architecture achieves robust data protection with only limited performance impact, successfully reconciling the conflicting requirements of speed and security.

[arXiv260629] P-ARC: Exploiting Subproblem Independence for Parallel Multi-Robot Motion Planning

arXiv
aimulti-robot motion planningparallel motion planning, adaptive robot coordination, OR-parallel multi-start, conflict detection, conflict resolution, subproblem independence
Authors
James D. Motes, Marco Morales, Nancy M. Amato
Institution
University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign, Instituto Tecnológico Autónomo de México

This paper introduces P-ARC, a parallel variant of the Adaptive Robot Coordination (ARC) approach for multi-robot motion planning. P-ARC parallelizes three key stages of ARC—initial individual planning, conflict detection, and conflict resolution—by exploiting subproblem independence, and also incorporates an OR-parallel multi-start strategy. Evaluations show speedups of up to 4X over the sequential version for large robot teams using 16 CPU cores, demonstrating the effectiveness of task decomposition for parallelism in motion planning.

[arXiv260629] DiStash: A Disaggregated Multi-Stash Transactional Key-Value Store

arXiv
sysdisaggregated storage, transactional key-value storedisaggregated storage, transactional key-value store, stash, FoundationDB, ephemeral storage, durable storage, inclusive/exclusive data placement
Authors
Yiming Gao, Hieu Nguyen, Jun Li, Shahram Ghandeharizadeh
Institution
University of Southern California, eBay Inc.

DiStash is a disaggregated transactional key-value store built on FoundationDB that manages data across multiple stash types (DRAM, SSD, HDD, NVM) using single transactions. It prevents race conditions and data loss across different stash pools and supports both ephemeral and durable storage modes. The system was evaluated with microbenchmarks and eBay’s production workload, demonstrating tradeoffs in design decisions.

[arXiv260629] Lightweight Multi-Vehicle Collaborative Perception Acceleration with Fusion Position Adjustment

arXiv
mlsysmulti-modal inferenceintermediate-fusion, conditional additivity, fusion position adjustment, feature fusion, inference acceleration, edge intelligence
Authors
Wenzhao Zhang, Shujun Han, Haixiao Gao, Mengying Sun, Bizhu Wang, Xiaodong Xu
Institution
Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications, Peng Cheng Laboratory

This paper proposes a lightweight acceleration scheme for intermediate-fusion multi-vehicle collaborative perception (MvCP) by adjusting the fusion position among linear layers. The authors introduce a relaxed definition of conditional additivity and prove that such adjustments maintain precision consistency, enabling theoretical acceleration without accuracy loss. Simulation results show up to 74.8% latency reduction under limited communication and 30.3% under limited computation resources.

[arXiv260629] How far does a random forest generalize from a 54-run LAMMPS+SPICA benchmark?

arXiv
mlsysothersrandom forest, molecular dynamics, LAMMPS, SPICA, MPI+OpenMP, HPC configuration tuning, feature importance, leave-one-dimension-out generalization
Authors
Dennis Alves Pedersen, Paulo Henrique Leme Ramalho, Fábio Andrijauskas
Institution
University of São Francisco

This paper investigates using a cold-start Random Forest surrogate, trained on just 54 LAMMPS+SPICA benchmark runs, to predict execution performance and recommend high-performing hybrid MPI+OpenMP configurations for molecular dynamics workloads. The surrogate accurately predicts loop time with 4.0% relative error in-sample, but its generalization is governed by hardware regime membership, performing well within the same regime while degrading across architectural boundaries. The result is an interpretable map of trusted recommendations, enabling scoping of further benchmark campaigns at a fraction of the cost.

[arXiv260629] Optimizing Teacher-Student Partitioning for Scalable Knowledge Distillation on HPC Systems

arXiv
mlsysllm trainingknowledge distillation, teacher-student asymmetry, topology-aware parallelism, vertical partitioning, horizontal partitioning, memory footprint optimization
Authors
Adrian P. Dieguez, Victor Conchello Vendrell, Alex Batlle, Vinnam Kim, Jordi Ros-Giralt, Harris Teague
Institution
Qualcomm AI Research (Qualcomm Technologies, Inc.)

This paper proposes an HPC-aware methodology for knowledge distillation that decouples teacher and student model partitioning to exploit their asymmetry in memory and communication requirements. By combining vertical and horizontal partitioning and deriving an analytical expression for optimal splitting regimes, the approach achieves up to 67% higher samples-per-second compared to the TRL library. The results demonstrate that topology-aware parallelism significantly accelerates generalized knowledge distillation training on production HPC clusters.

[arXiv260629] Exploring and Exploiting Synchrony Limitations of Time-Triggered Network-Agnostic Guardians

arXiv
sysreal-time systems, securityclock synchronization, timing attack, bus guardian, FlexRay, OMNeT++, TDMA
Authors
Shreya Vithal Kulhalli, Mohammad Ibrahim Alkoudsi, Gerhard Fohler
Institution
RPTU Kaiserslautern-Landau

The paper derives a theoretical lower bound on clock synchronization precision between a node and its network-agnostic guardian. It then introduces a timing attack that exploits this unavoidable synchrony limitation to cause an undetected de-synchronization, enabling a malicious node to disrupt critical network messages via collisions. Simulations on a FlexRay model show the attack achieves 100% undetected success and 100% disruption of targeted critical messages.
