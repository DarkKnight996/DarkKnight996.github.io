[arXiv260713] SiFAR: Synchronization-Free All-Reduce for Low-Latency LLM Inference

arXiv
mlsysllm inferencesynchronization-free all-reduce, tensor parallelism, in-switch reduction, dual buffering, speculative reduction, redundant pull
Authors
Hritvik Taneja, Anish Saxena, Abhishek Revinipati, Jae Hyung Ju, Neal C. Crago, Moinuddin Qureshi
Institution
Georgia Institute of Technology, NVIDIA

This paper proposes SiFAR, a synchronization-free all-reduce algorithm for low-latency LLM inference. It eliminates the bottom and top barriers in existing all-reduce methods through dual buffering, redundant pull using in-switch reduction, and speculative reduction. SiFAR reduces all-reduce latency by up to 52% and improves end-to-end inference throughput by 18.6% for Llama-3.1-8B on 8 GPUs.

[arXiv260713] Wireless Decentralized Federated Learning via Device Clustering and Inter-Cluster Link Enhancement

arXiv
mlsyscluster infrastructuredecentralized federated learning, device clustering, inter-cluster link enhancement, backhaul provisioning, over-the-air computation, gossip algorithm
Authors
William Weijia Zheng, Hang Liu, Ying-Jun Angela Zhang
Institution
The Chinese University of Hong Kong, University of Macau

The paper proposes a budget-aware, cluster-centric decentralized federated learning (DFL) framework for wireless D2D networks. It partitions the network into dense clusters and allocates limited backhaul links to cluster heads, enabling fast intra-cluster aggregation and infrequent inter-cluster exchanges. Experiments show this approach accelerates convergence with only a few strategic backhaul links.

[arXiv260713] New Complexity Classes in Locally Checkable Labeling for Local Computation Algorithms

arXiv
aidistributed algorithmsLocal Computation Algorithms, Locally Checkable Labeling, VOLUME model, complexity classes, probe complexity
Authors
Sijin Peng
Institution
MIT

This paper studies Local Computation Algorithms (LCAs) for Locally Checkable Labeling (LCL) problems, aiming to understand their complexity classifications. The authors construct LCLs with probe complexities of Θ(log^k n) and Θ̃(n^{p/q}) in the VOLUME model, generalizing to LCAs by stacking instances of different complexities. Their approach, distinct from prior work, provides new complexity classes for these sublinear algorithms.

[arXiv260713] STEEL: Sparsity-Aware Fused Attention for Energy-Efficient Long-Sequence Inference on AMD's XDNA NPU

arXiv
mlsysllm inferenceflash attention, sparsity-aware pipeline, dataflow formulation, prefill attention, XDNA NPU, energy-efficient inference
Authors
Victor J.B. Jung, Gagandeep Singh, Joseph Melber, Kristof Denolf, Francesco Conti, Luca Benini
Institution
AMD Research and Advanced Development, ETH Zurich, University of Bologna

The paper presents STEEL, the first open-source FlashAttention implementation for AMD XDNA NPUs, which uses a novel dataflow formulation for prefill attention to exploit spatial parallelism and a sparsity-aware pipeline placement to mitigate load imbalance from causal masks. Evaluated on the AMD Ryzen AI 9 HX 370 SoC, STEEL reduces energy consumption by up to 9.17x over CPU and 1.75x over GPU baselines, and achieves significant latency improvements over prior state-of-the-art on XDNA 1 and XDNA 2.

[arXiv260713] Bidirectional Resource Scheduling for Disaggregated and Asynchronous RL Post-Training

arXiv
mlsyspost-trainingbidirectional resource scheduling, disaggregated architecture, asynchronous RL, hot-switch runtime, time-performance modeling, resource idleness reduction
Authors
Tan Zhiqiang, Wang Maoxin, Wang Sijie, Yin Yiming, Wang Qiang, Chu Xiaowen, Shi Shaohuai
Institution
Harbin Institute of Technology, Shenzhen; The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou)

This paper presents BiDiRL, a hybrid time-space multiplexing architecture for asynchronous, disaggregated reinforcement learning post-training that reduces resource idleness. It introduces a hot-switch runtime for rapid resource switching, a static scheduling-aware planner for balanced resource partitioning, and a bidirectional scheduler for fine-grained runtime resource borrowing. Experiments on 32-GPU testbeds show BiDiRL improves RL training throughput by up to 1.94x compared to existing systems without affecting convergence.

[arXiv260713] Proof-of-Continuity: A Temporal Model for Authority Propagation in Distributed Systems and AI Agents

arXiv
sysauthorizationProof-of-Continuity, Provenance Identity Continuity, Proof-of-Possession, causal authority propagation, confused deputy
Authors
Nicola Gallo
Institution
Nicola Gallo (no institutional affiliation provided)

This paper introduces Proof-of-Continuity, an authorization propagation model that requires each execution step to be causally linked to its origin, preventing the confused deputy problem in distributed systems and AI agents. It complements Proof-of-Possession by ensuring that later steps can only exercise a non-expansive subset of the original authority, without merging multiple authority sources. The model's key primitive is Proof-of-Relationship, a single-hop causal link, whose transitive composition forms Proof-of-Continuity.

[arXiv260713] HERO: A Heterogeneity-Aware Benchmark Library for Federated Continual Learning

arXiv
mlsysothersfederated continual learning, benchmark library, class-incremental learning, domain-incremental learning, task-order mismatch, data skew, replay, knowledge distillation, prompt tuning
Authors
Thinh T. H. Nguyen, Le-Tuan Nguyen, Minh-Duong Nguyen, Nhi Trinh, Anh Tran Nam Nguyet, Dung D. Le, Kok-Seng Wong
Institution
VinUniversity

The paper introduces HERO, a heterogeneity-aware benchmark library for Federated Continual Learning (FCL) that decouples task splits, client data splits, and client task sequences to enable fair comparisons. The benchmark uses alpha and rho parameters to control data skew and task-order mismatch, and evaluates methods on CIFAR-100, TinyImageNet, and a graph-based domain-incremental case study. Key findings show that average accuracy can hide weak client performance, task-order mismatch favors different strategies, and the same interface can expose domain-shift difficulty beyond image-based FCL.

[arXiv260713] Distributed Symmetry Breaking on Hyperbolic Random Graphs

arXiv
aidistributed algorithmshyperbolic random graphs, maximal independent set, maximal matching, LOCAL model, lower bounds, algorithms
Authors
Yannic Maus, Janosch Ruff, Sonia Simons, George Skretas
Institution
TU Graz, Hasso Plattner Institute, University of Potsdam

This paper studies distributed symmetry-breaking problems on hyperbolic random graphs (HRGs), proving that maximal independent set (MIS) and maximal matching (MM) require Ω(log log n / log log log n) rounds, unlike Δ+1 coloring which can be solved in 2 rounds. The authors also design algorithms tailored to HRGs that solve MIS and MM in Õ(log^{5/3} log n) rounds, nearly matching these lower bounds, and show that geometric information can further reduce MM complexity to O(log log log n) rounds. The results highlight fundamental differences in distributed problem difficulty on realistic network models.

[arXiv260713] Short Graph Sketches Suffice for Error-resilient Leader Verification in CONGEST

arXiv
sysdistributed computing, error-resilient verificationlocal graph sketches, imagined trees, imagined certifications, CONGEST model, locally checkable proofs with errors (LCPE), view distance, leader verification
Authors
Pawel Garncarek, Tomasz Jurdziński, Dariusz Kowalski, Subhajit Pramanick
Institution
University of Wrocław, Poland; Augusta University, USA

This paper introduces efficient CONGEST model implementations for locally checkable proofs with errors (LCPE) by focusing on the unique-leader verification problem on trees. The authors develop local graph sketches that encode essential verification information in only O(ε² log n) bits per node, enabling tolerance of up to ε adversarial certificate errors within O(ε²) communication rounds. They also prove a matching impossibility result showing that any verification scheme with view distance at most ε cannot tolerate ε errors, even in the more powerful LOCAL model, confirming the necessity of their approach.

[arXiv260713] Hybrid Quantum and Classical Workload Management with Graph-based Scheduling

arXiv
sysworkload schedulinggang scheduling, graph-based scheduling, quantum-classical workflows, two-queue problem, cloud-native scheduler, Kubernetes, Fluxion
Authors
Vanessa Sochat, Daniel Milroy
Institution
Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory

The paper presents Fluence, a Kubernetes scheduler plugin that extends the Fluxion graph-based scheduler to enable informed, gang-scheduled placement for hybrid quantum-classical workloads. It addresses the "two-queue problem" of managing both a traditional scheduler queue and a remote quantum device queue, achieving near-elimination of wasted node-time under contention, roughly 5x reduction in worker idle time, and roughly 70x cost reduction through cost- and queue-aware backend selection. The results demonstrate that quantum awareness can be added to a cloud-native scheduler without modifying user containers.
