[arXiv260831] FISGuard: Defending Against Membership Inference via Fixed Input Subspaces

arXiv

Authors
Haocheng Jiang, Hua Shen
Institution
Hubei University of Technology

FISGuard is a lightweight defense against ProjRes membership inference attacks in federated large language model fine-tuning. The paper introduces juFFTe, a high-performance FFT library optimized for RISC-V processors with vector extensions (RVV 1.0), demonstrating effective vectorization of FFT kernels. [arXiv260831] TerraceMoE: A Cost Model for Hierarchical MoE All-to-All Communication

arXiv

Authors
Weicheng Xue, Bingqiang Wang, Li Yuan, Huihui Zhou, Yonghong Tian
Institution
Virginia Tech, Peking University

The paper presents TerraceMoE, a communication-level cost model to evaluate whether hierarchical two-hop dispatch in expert-parallel Mixture-of-Experts training is beneficial, considering fabric bandwidth ratios and arrival-chain overheads. Through validation gates and calibration on real platforms, it finds the breakeven hierarchy ratio is highly implementation- and platform-dependent, and that current measured systems do not yet reach the hierarchical regime, thus avoiding overconfident training-throughput predictions.

The paper benchmarks the real-world HPC performance of the RISC-V Vector Extension (RVV) 1.0 across four latest commercially available processors (SiFive X280, SpacemiT X60/X100, T-Head C920v2) using standard HPC workloads (BLAS, FFTW, HPL, HPCG, STREAM) and compares them against the NVIDIA Grace ARM64 chip. This paper presents HARTS, a system for efficient agentic reinforcement learning on hybrid-attention models by exploiting prefix sharing in rollout trees. [arXiv260831] Characterization of Request and Token Energy Costs for LLM Inference Workloads on GPU Platforms

arXiv
mlsysllm inferenceenergy model, token energy, request energy, GPU, H100, H200, batching, mixture-of-experts, prefill, decode
Authors
Prabhu Vellaisamy, Vanessa Lam, Shawn Blanton, John Paul Shen
Institution
Carnegie Mellon University

The paper proposes a decomposed energy model for LLM inference on GPUs, separating fixed prefill/setup costs from per-token generation energy. Through experiments on H100 and H200 across dense and MoE models, it shows that token-normalized energy can decline while total request energy rises, and that batching and MoE routing affect this tradeoff. The authors conclude that energy-aware serving must jointly optimize request energy and token energy rather than per-token cost alone.

The paper proposes Learning-Augmented Heuristics (LAH), a framework that learns cache-level parameters for static eviction heuristics by decoupling data and control planes. Authors

Institution

The paper optimizes GPU execution of two wave-based non-line-of-sight (NLOS) reconstruction algorithms, f-k migration and phasor-fields, for streaming and offline processing. Key innovations include offline kernel assembly via analytic Fourier transforms, fused kernels, warp-level photon binning, batched transforms, CUDA graph replay, and selective FP16 storage, resulting in up to 42x speedup and 40x memory reduction over baselines. The authors also propose three denoising strategies enabled by the improved frame budget for next-generation NLOS video processing.

This paper reviews AI hardware accelerators for large language models, analyzing architectures like GPUs, ASICs, FPGAs, and processing-in-memory systems using roofline analysis and transformer structure. [arXiv260831] Relaxed Sender Anonymity for CBDC Interbank Settlement: A Zero-Knowledge Approach on Permissioned EVM

Authors

Institution

The paper proposes a confidential interbank settlement protocol for CBDC on a permissioned Ethereum-compatible network, using Groth16 zero-knowledge proofs, Poseidon hash commitments in an incremental Merkle tree, and ECIES-encrypted notes stored on-chain to hide receiver identity, transfer amount, and business payload while keeping the sender public for compliance.
