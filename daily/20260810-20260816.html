[arXiv260810] Cascade: Exploiting SLO-Aware latency budget for fair and high goodput LLM inference serving

arXiv
mlsysllm inferenceSLO-aware scheduling, latency budget, KV-cache management, prefetching, recomputation, goodput optimization, fairness, vLLM
Authors
Muhammad Adnan, Rohan Mahapatra, Prashant J. Nair, Daniel Berger, Pantea Zardoshti, Rodrigo Fonseca, Esha Choukse
Institution
The University of British Columbia, Microsoft Azure Research, NVIDIA

Cascade is an LLM inference serving system that uses a per-request latency budget (SLO minus predicted remaining service time) to jointly coordinate request scheduling and KV-cache management across memory tiers. By prioritizing requests with low remaining budget and deciding whether to prefetch, retain, or recompute KV state based on this budget, Cascade improves SLO-satisfied goodput by up to 2.4x and reduces SLO violations by 40% compared to vLLM's default FCFS scheduler while preserving fairness.

Authors
Yash Kurkure, Yihe Zhang, Zhiling Lan, Michael E. Papka
Institution
University of Illinois Chicago, Argonne National Laboratory

The paper introduces MARS, a training-free HPC scheduler that uses Monte Carlo Tree Search with a lightweight discrete-event simulator to explore future scheduling decisions within a time budget. Its optimization goal is configurable via a reward function, allowing operators to switch between wait-time minimization and utilization maximization without retraining. Evaluated on production workloads from Theta and Polaris, MARS outperforms heuristics and DRL by proactively planning around reservations, reducing tail wait time by up to 64% and recovering utilization before maintenance windows.

[arXiv260810] Rendezvous of Mobile Deterministic Automata in Graphs

arXiv
sysdistributed computingrendezvous, deterministic finite automata, mobile agents, pebbles, anonymous graphs, tree graphs
Authors
Bibhuti Das, Andrzej Pelc
Institution
Université du Québec en Outaouais

This paper studies the rendezvous problem for identical deterministic finite automata (DFA) agents moving in synchronous rounds on unlabeled graphs, where agents may start at different times. The authors prove that without node marking, no DFA can guarantee rendezvous even on a line, and that with any finite number of pebbles, universal rendezvous is impossible for general graphs. Their main result is that for tree graphs, a single movable pebble per agent suffices to design a universal DFA, while stationary pebbles of any finite number are insufficient.

This paper proposes Stream Learning, a set of gossip learning protocols inspired by peer-to-peer live streaming, where model partitions are treated as video chunks and partition age as scarcity.
