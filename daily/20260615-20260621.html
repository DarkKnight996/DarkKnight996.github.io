[arXiv260615] Solving Subgraph Extraction Problems Using Search

arXiv
aicombinatorial optimization, heuristic searchsubgraph extraction, reward-penalty optimization, delta debugging, n-way parallelism
Authors
Rebin Silva Valan Arasu, Rajiv Gupta
Institution
University of California Riverside

The paper introduces ΔSearch, a general heuristic framework for solving NP-hard subgraph extraction problems by using a reward-penalty optimization approach. It requires only feasibility constraints and optimality criteria from the user, and can also improve exact methods via aggressive pruning. The framework matches or surpasses state-of-the-art heuristics on several problems without problem-specific tuning.

[arXiv260615] Selective Field Transmission: Bandwidth Efficient Communication under Standardized Message Schemas

arXiv
sysmiddleware, communication optimizationSelective Field Transmission, publish-subscribe, DDS, bandwidth reduction, serialization optimization, ROS 2
Authors
David Philipp Klüner, David Murach, Stefan Kowalewski, Alexandru Kampmann
Institution
RWTH Aachen University

Selective Field Transmission (SFT) is a middleware mechanism that dynamically adapts transmitted message components to each receiver's actual needs in publish-subscribe systems. It resolves the trade-off between using standard message types for interoperability and optimizing bandwidth, by allowing receivers to declare required fields for selective transmission. The evaluation shows significant bandwidth reductions without measurable per-message latency overhead.

[arXiv260615] Lattice Aggregation in Distributed Verification under Crash and Byzantine Failures

arXiv
sysdistributed verificationc-Lattice Aggregation, crash failures, Byzantine failures, SCD-broadcast, indistinguishability-based lower bounds, globally dependent languages
Authors
Gilde Valeria Rodríguez, Borzoo Bonakdarpour, Armando Castañeda, Sergio Rajsbaum
Institution
Universidad Nacional Autónoma de México, Michigan State University

This paper introduces c-Lattice Aggregation, a fault-tolerant reconstruction problem for distributed verification under crash and Byzantine failures, where processes must collaboratively reconstruct a concurrent execution from partial samples. The authors establish tight feasibility thresholds for solving the problem based on a redundancy parameter and provide matching algorithms and lower bounds. The work also defines globally dependent languages and proves that sound monitoring requires solving c-Lattice Aggregation, offering the first complete characterization of fault-tolerant verification under Byzantine failures.

This paper presents VaultxGPU, a GPU-accelerated extension of the VaultX Proof-of-Space plotter that offloads Blake3 hashing to GPUs using custom CUDA and SYCL kernels, keeping data in GPU VRAM and fusing sort and match stages to minimize data movement. [arXiv260615] On the Limits of Causal Observation in Shared-Memory Systems

arXiv
sysdistributed systems, concurrent algorithmscausal observability, linearizability, quiescent consistency, timestamping, instrumentation placement, non-blocking implementations, NUMA, cache-contention
Authors
Gilde Valeria Rodríguez, Armando Castañeda, Miguel Piña
Institution
Universidad Nacional Autónoma de México

The paper formalizes the Causal Observability Problem (COP) to determine operation order in concurrent systems, proving that a complete and sound timestamp solution is impossible at the observable boundary. It shows that the placement of instrumentation events (internal vs. external) determines whether completeness or soundness can be guaranteed, independent of the timestamp mechanism. Three implementations (FAInc, Striped, Collect) validate that placement alone governs observable behavior, with Striped achieving high throughput and linearizability on a 64-core NUMA architecture.

Institution

The paper proposes Simple-IT, a new leader-based, signature-free Byzantine fault-tolerant (BFT) consensus protocol that achieves low latency (4 message delays, 3 on optimistic path) without relying on quantum-vulnerable public-key signatures. Authors

This paper presents an eight-week longitudinal study of silent failures in a production LLM agent runtime, documenting 22 incidents with root-cause postmortems. The authors derive a five-class taxonomy of silent failures, identifying "fail-plausible" behavior where LLMs transform errors into fluent narratives, and find that about 70% of such failures were caught by human observation, not automated tests.
