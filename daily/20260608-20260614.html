Institution

PCCL proposes a scalable and generic framework for synthesizing topology-aware collective algorithms for distributed machine learning. Authors
Victory C. Obieke
Institution

The paper studies communication strategies for multi-GPU 3D FDTD with CPML boundary layers, comparing host-staged exchange, direct GPU-to-GPU peer exchange, and enlarged ghost regions. The main finding is that direct GPU-to-GPU peer exchange provides the dominant speedup (2.46–2.76× over host-staged), while enlarged ghost regions yield only modest additional benefits due to redundant computation and memory traffic overhead.

The paper proposes a hierarchical mission-level runtime assurance framework for autonomous driving that jointly evaluates platform safety and future mission feasibility before executing high-level commands. [arXiv260608] P-Cast Precision in FP8 Attention: Sink-Induced Collapse and the Optimality of S=2^8

arXiv

Authors
Reed Lau
Institution
Tencent

This paper analyzes precision challenges in FP8 (E4M3) attention, specifically when the softmax matrix P is cast to FP8 before the P·V matrix multiplication. It finds that forward KV iteration causes "P-collapse," where non-sink attention values underflow to zero due to the attention sink phenomenon, and proposes using reverse iteration combined with a static scaling factor of S=256 to fully prevent this underflow. The authors provide a quantitative theoretical framework and demonstrate 3-10x MSE improvements in kernel-level experiments.

[arXiv260608] Hierarchical Certified Semantic Commitment for Byzantine-Resilient LLM-Agent Collaboration

arXiv
mlsysfault-tolerancehierarchical certified semantic commitment, Byzantine fault tolerance, LLM agents, typed commit, semantic commit, verdict commit, safe abort
Authors
Haoran Xu, Lei Zhang, Iadh Ounis, Xianbin Wang
Institution
University of Glasgow, University of Western Ontario

The paper proposes Hierarchical Certified Semantic Commitment (H-CSC), a Byzantine fault tolerance-inspired protocol for LLM-agent collaboration that converts embedding-derived finality signals into one of three typed outcomes: semantic_commit, verdict_commit, or an explicit abort. Evaluated on controlled and real benchmarks, H-CSC achieves low angular deviation, 100% abort on infeasible rounds, and high commit rates with low invalid rates, while providing typed semantic provenance that verdict-only baselines lack. The verdict-level fallback is shown to be necessary for coverage without compromising safety.
